# Re-analysis of the 2026-07-26 runs — control-plane delivery and gap geometry

Date: 2026-07-27. Boards powered off; this is a pure re-analysis of the archived
logs in `bench-evidence/radio_monitor_20260726_*/`, triggered by a dispute over
the reverse-slot geometry during the single-radio architecture analysis.

**It overturned two things we believed:** the "171/1 command convergence"
headline (a measurement artifact — see the retraction in TODO.md RS-2.2) and

## Probe: fragment-timing

DISPUTE 2 RESOLVED — the critic is right about the direction and understates the problem. At DTS with the reverse slot enabled, the median inter-fragment dead air is 4.98 ms, not 44 ms and not 12.7 ms. The gap distribution is bimodal, not centred on a mean: 93.9% of inter-fragment gaps are ~4.98 ms (fit nothing — not the 15.42 ms bare frame, not the 10.30 ms frame the base actually sends today), and 6.1% are the once-per-frame train boundary at ~85–134 ms (fit all three candidates). The "~12.7 ms" figure is the arithmetic MEAN of that bimodal distribution and describes no gap that actually occurs. Direct air evidence closes it: in the 15-min record run the base transmitted 94 command frames during saturation and the tractor received ZERO.

### Implications

- ANSWER TO THE HEADLINE QUESTION: a 15.42 ms bare control frame does NOT fit in the inter-fragment dead time at DTS. It fits in 6.1% of gaps (the once-per-frame train boundary at 85–134 ms) and in 0% of the remaining 93.9%. The 20.54 ms D13-authenticated frame: same 6.1%. The 25.66 ms GCM-128 frame: same 6.1%. All three have IDENTICAL fit fractions, because the gap distribution has a hole between 5 ms and 85 ms — there is nothing for a 15 vs 20 vs 26 ms distinction to bite on. Choosing a cheaper crypto profile buys exactly zero additional delivery opportunities. Do not spend engineering effort on D13 or tag truncation to solve this problem; it is not a frame-size problem.
- THE 40 ms TRAIN GAP DOES NOT APPEAR AS A DISTINCT MODE, and that is because it is not a separate event — it is a floor inside a larger boundary window. Expected once per completed frame; in the record run that is once per 17 fragments (13 data + 4 parity from parity_group=4), not once per 13 — 6.1% of gaps, one every 1.85 s. The measured boundary window is 85–134 ms, i.e. 2–3× the designed 40 ms, so the reverse slot is entirely contained within pre-existing host-side train-prep time. Confirmed by the ON/OFF comparison at matched geometry: enabling tx_prepare_ahead did not lengthen the boundary (183.1 ms vs 186.5–192.6 ms dt_p95). The slot costs no throughput. It also did not, by itself, make commands land.
- BOTH DISPUTED FIGURES ARE WRONG, IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS. The architecture analysis's ~44 ms is stale by one day and over-stated by 13 ms: it comes from 2026-07-25 Run D, where the correct arithmetic gives 30.6 ms, not 44 ms (NOTES.md line 112 dropped the 8 B hop header from the ToA). The critic's ~12.7 ms is arithmetically correct but is the MEAN of a hard bimodal distribution, and no gap of 12.7 ms occurs anywhere in the run. The number that governs the architecture decision is neither: it is 4.98 ms for 93.9% of gaps and ~85–134 ms for 6.1%.
- THE 44 ms WAS NOT LOST — IT WAS SPENT, DELIBERATELY. Between 07-25 and 07-26 the throughput program (RS-3.1 batching, v3 depth-2 pipelining, RS-3.10 prepare-ahead) converted per-fragment host turnaround into payload: goodput 1745→2046 B/s (+17%) while per-fragment dead air fell 30.6→4.98 ms (−84%). That is the actual trade the operator is being asked to ratify. Throughput and control-plane latency on this single radio are the same budget line, and the record 2046 B/s number was bought with the control window.
- THE AIR MEASUREMENT IS WORSE THAN THE GAP ARITHMETIC PREDICTS, AND IT IS THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE IN THE ARCHIVE. Gap geometry predicts ~6% of wall time in usable windows; observed in-stream command delivery in the 15-min record run was 0 of 94. In the 30-min run it was 6 of 255 (2.4%), all six clustered in a single 1.5 s burst — i.e. delivered by a stream stall, not by a train-boundary window. Whatever the modelled window is, base→tractor commands empirically did not get through at DTS. An E-stop on this path in this configuration would have been dropped.
- MECHANISM, FROM CODE, FOR WHY THE 4.98 ms IS WORSE THAN IT LOOKS: in the v3 pipelined path that all these runs used (tx_pipeline=v3), the tractor never re-arms RXCONT between fragments. The _ensure_rxcont + _drain_rx_frames pair lives only in the serial v2 path (firmware/tractor_x8/image_tx_daemon.py:894-900); the pipelined path (:926-1005) has no per-fragment re-arm, and the depth-2 firmware mailbox means fragment N+1 is already parked in the L072 while N radiates. So those 4.98 ms are STANDBY turnaround, not armed listening. The usable listening time is the train boundary alone — 4.6% of wall clock in the record run — plus stalls.
- IF THE SINGLE-RADIO ARCHITECTURE IS TO PROCEED, the lever is scheduling, not crypto and not PHY. Two concrete options the evidence supports: (a) enlarge and guarantee the boundary window — it is already 85–134 ms and costs nothing today, so a hard-scheduled reverse slot of 30–40 ms with the modem verifiably in RXCONT would give one deterministic control opportunity every 1.85 s; (b) close the phase loop — the base already knows exactly when the window opens because it just received the frame's last fragment, and NOTES.md line 116 flags this as queued RS-1.x work that was never landed. Note (a) alone still yields a 1.85 s worst-case single-shot latency, which is a number the operator must accept explicitly for an E-stop.
- FOR THE E-STOP SPECIFICALLY, the evidence does not support carrying it on this link as currently scheduled. Even granting a perfect boundary window, the control-plane cadence is one opportunity per 1.85 s and measured in-stream delivery was 0–2.4% per attempt. If the operator's ruling out of a second radio stands, the honest options are: hard-preempt the image stream on E-stop assertion (the tractor stops transmitting, which is the correct failure direction anyway), or invert the safety logic to a heartbeat the tractor must HEAR to keep moving — which turns a delivery problem into a timeout problem the link can actually satisfy.

### Caveats

- The air_gap log line emits only median and p95 per 10 s window (base_station/image_rx_daemon.py:1139-1145) — the raw per-fragment deltas are not archived. Mode identification is therefore inferential. The 4.98 ms intra-train figure is solid (it is a median over a class comprising 93.9% of samples, reproduced across 8 runs and 6 git SHAs). The 85 ms boundary figure is weaker: it rests on the p95 rank coinciding with the boundary frequency (5.9% vs 5.88%) in the 17-fragment record run, which is a fortunate coincidence and does NOT hold for the runs with 2–5 fragments/frame, where p95 sits deep in the boundary tail and cannot be interpreted the same way. Treat 85–134 ms as a range, not a measurement.
- dt measures RX-to-RX (firmware DIO0/RX_DONE timestamp, firmware/murata_l072/radio/sx1276_rx.c:98), not the tractor's TX-start-to-TX-start. A fragment lost on air merges two gaps into one, and 4.3% of TX'd fragments were never received in the record run (7911 TX'd vs 7515 sampled). The dt_p95 mode at 210–219 ms is exactly 2×104.88 and is a LOSS artifact — it is NOT a listening window and must not be counted as one. This is the single largest way to over-read this data.
- Method 3 is the less trustworthy of the two, for a reason that also explains part of its 12.58 vs 4.98 divergence: it assumes every fragment is len_med, but the last data fragment of each frame is a runt (88 B in the record run, ToA 41.02 ms not 99.90 ms). Applying it naively to the short 2–3 fragment/frame runs produces physically impossible NEGATIVE dead time (−3.96 ms in run 083837), which is how the bias was caught. The runt-corrected exact reconstruction (88.6% duty) is what should be quoted, not the uniform-length version (88.8%). Method 2's median is immune because 247 B is the modal length.
- params.txt records the STARTING regulatory profile, not the profile the radio ran. Run 133257 is labelled reg_profile=1 but switched to profile 2 in-run (its rx_daemon.log logs 'radio_profile: commanding tractor 1 -> 2' at 18:30:47). Any analysis keyed on params.txt reg_profile alone will mis-assign its ToA. The dt − ToA ≥ 0 sanity check is what caught it and should be applied to any future run classification.
- The premise handed to me needs one correction: train_gap_ms=40 appears explicitly in params.txt only from run 125943 onward (9 runs), not 10 from 12:50. Run 125048 has tx_prepare_ahead=1 with no train_gap_ms line. The conclusion still holds, but for a different reason than stated — TRAIN_GAP_S DEFAULTS to 40 ms (firmware/tractor_x8/image_tx_daemon.py:175, os.environ.get('LIFETRAC_TRAIN_GAP_MS', '40')), so the slot was live in 125048 too, and in fact in every run where tx_prepare_ahead=1 regardless of whether the harness set the variable.
- Run 163103 (30-min, profile 2) is unusable for gap analysis: only 2 air_gap lines survive, with len_med=53 B and dt_med 400–792 ms — the image stream was not saturated during the sampled windows. Its tx-side stats are fine and were used for method 3 only.
- The 0-of-94 delivery result rests on the tractor logging every command it parses. I verified image_tx_daemon.py:603-628 has no dedup or rate-limit at the log sites for ENCODE_MODE, REQ_KEYFRAME, RADIO_PROFILE or RADIO_PROFILE_CONF, so a received-but-unlogged frame is not possible for those opcodes. It would be possible for an opcode with no log line, but none of the 94 in-stream transmissions can be shown to be such — the base's own transmit-side accounting (cmd_tx_ok/cmd_tx_fail) does not record which opcode each frame carried, so the opcode mix of those 94 is not recoverable from these logs.
- The 17.5% figure that a naive 20-of-114 reading produces is wrong and I want it explicitly killed, because it is the easiest mistake to make from these logs: all 20 of the tractor's receptions in run 174656 predate the stream by 11 seconds. Any comparison of cmd_tx_ok against reception counts must first partition on the first frame-done timestamp.
- This is archived-log analysis with hardware powered off. Nothing here was re-run or re-measured, and the 85–134 ms boundary range in particular would be settled in one 5-minute bench run by logging the raw gap samples (or a histogram) instead of median/p95 — that change is a two-line edit at image_rx_daemon.py:1133-1145.

### Implications

- DISPUTE 2 is settled AGAINST the architecture analysis, and the critic understated it. The measured per-fragment listening window at DTS is 5.01 ms, not 44 ms and not 12.7 ms. Neither the 10.30 ms command actually used nor the 15.42 ms 16 B ControlFrame fits between fragments. Any duty/gap arithmetic in the analysis that assumed a 44 ms per-fragment window must be rebuilt.
- The 44 ms figure was never a per-fragment number. NOTES.md:120-123 defines it as per-FRAME host-daemon overhead (Run D, 2026-07-25), and by RS-3.1 batching that overhead is amortized across 2-4 frames per train. The analysis appears to have promoted a per-train host-overhead number into a per-fragment radio window.
- There IS a real inter-TRAIN window — 92 ms every 451 ms at the soak, 240 ms every 1.87 s at the record run — geometrically large enough for a drive frame. The failure is not physics, it is scheduling: with the reverse slot enabled the base hit that window 0 times in 386 tries when chance alone predicts ~45 (P = 1.8e-22 for the soak alone). The base's completion-aligned pump is firing systematically INTO the tractor's transmissions.
- The '171 delivered / 1 give-up' number must be withdrawn from the architecture argument entirely. It counts keyframes arriving, not commands landing. The tractor heard 6 REQ_KEYFRAME in 30 minutes, and all 6 landed only because a firmware FAULT (code=0x0B) stalled its transmitter for 3.19 s. 61 of the 171 'convergences' fired with zero commands ever transmitted. Nothing about base->tractor command delivery can be inferred from it, and the derived p50/p90/p95 (1.5/5.7/7.3 s) are measurements of the tractor's spontaneous keyframe cadence.
- The reverse slot as shipped is a REGRESSION, not an improvement. Run J (084712) — same 75% util, same profile 2, but WITHOUT prepare_ahead/train_gap/parity and with 255 ms copy spacing — achieved 14/24 = 58% in-stream per-copy delivery, 3.2x chance. Every run with train_gap_ms=40 enabled achieved 0. Whatever combination of TX_PREPARE_AHEAD, the 40 ms designed gap, and parity_group=4 was introduced between those runs destroyed the alignment that Run J had won.
- Answer to the cadence/latency question at DTS: NONE is supported. With 0/386 measured in-stream deliveries, per-attempt delivery probability has a 95% upper bound of 0.78%. Even at Run J's best-ever 58%/copy you need 5.3 independent copies for 99% one-way delivery, at a >=255 ms copy cadence — ~1.35 s to land ONE command with 99% confidence, one-way, no ack. That is two orders of magnitude away from a hydraulic drive loop and is disqualifying for E-stop.
- Answer at FHSS: worse. 1 of 701 in-stream deliveries (0.14%), and only ~42% delivery even on an IDLE link because the two ends are not channel-aligned. FHSS cannot carry a control plane alongside the image stream at all on this evidence.
- The one configuration with measured reliable delivery is the one where the tractor is not transmitting: 66/66 copies at profile 2 on an idle link, and 6/6 during the fault stall. This points at a hard-muted reserved slot (the tractor's TX scheduler stops, guaranteed, on a known grid) rather than an opportunistic gap. The airtime cost is affordable — a 15.42 ms drive frame at 10 Hz is 154 ms/s = 15.4% of airtime, and yesterday's image stream already gives up 24% of wall time to dead air. The problem is enforcement and mutual timing knowledge, not airtime budget.
- A one-way drive command needs only the first leg, and the archive contains no clean measurement of it. The best single-clock number is a 254 ms idle-link round trip dominated by the tractor's 0.25 s queue-poll timeout (image_tx_daemon.py:478), not by air. Air is 10-15 ms. So a one-way command on a link where the tractor is actually listening should be tens of milliseconds — but that is an inference from code paths, not a measurement, and yesterday's runs never created the condition to measure it under load.
- Recommended next bench step (single run, answers the whole question): instrument the tractor to log a monotonic timestamp at each train boundary and stream that back, then have the base transmit a command at a KNOWN offset into the reverse slot rather than on frame-completion. Run the A/B against LIFETRAC_ALIGNED_PUMP=0 and against prepare_ahead=0 to isolate which of the three RS-3.10 changes killed Run J's 58%.

### Caveats

- The 92.4 ms per-boundary inter-train listening window is DERIVED, not directly logged. It depends on the host-accounted util figure (image_tx_daemon.py:1099, toa_us_sum/wall) being correct, on the 5.01 ms mid-train gap, and on frames_tx_ok being a per-TRAIN counter (confirmed at image_tx_daemon.py:1066-1069, which increments once per _tx_one_frame call). If util under-reports, the window is larger and the 0/386 result is even more anomalous.
- The base-side dt_p95 values (183-290 ms at profile 2) are a LOSS artifact, not a gap: the base missed ~7.7% of fragments in the soak (rx_frames 15238 vs tractor frags_ok 16501), and each miss doubles the apparent inter-arrival. Only dt_med is a trustworthy gap statistic. I did not use p95 for any conclusion.
- The 5.01 ms figure is a median of 179 per-10s medians, so it characterizes the typical mid-train gap. Because train boundaries are only ~1 in 4.15 fragments, the boundary gap is invisible in the median and cannot be read off the logged med/p95 pair — I had to reconstruct it from duty arithmetic.
- Cross-board clock skew is unquantified and non-zero. In 163103 and 133257 the tractor logs a command dispatch 30-50 ms AFTER the base has already received the resulting ack, which is causally impossible unless the tractor clock leads the base clock by >=50 ms. Every base-TX-to-tractor-log delta (n=210, p50=76 ms) is therefore inflated by an unknown amount and I have not used it as a latency measurement.
- radio_monitor_20260726_163103_72f2b735 is a partially failed run: the stream stalls (rx_frames frozen at 28 for the last ~2 minutes) and it commands a profile 2->1 switch that later reverts. I included its 0/42 in the DTS aggregate; excluding it gives 0/344, which changes nothing.
- radio_monitor_20260726_133257_9a633f1c is labelled reg_profile=1 in params.txt but switches to profile 2 at 18:30:47, so its air_gap statistics (len_med 247, dt_med 104.85) are BW500 data. I excluded it from the profile-1 gap aggregate.
- No run yesterday exercised anything resembling a drive cadence. Every command was REQ_KEYFRAME (0x60), ENCODE_MODE (0x63), or RADIO_PROFILE (0x65), at <=3 Hz, with 2-byte bodies. The 15.42 ms figure for a 16 B ControlFrame is pure arithmetic from lora_proto.py:505-524 — it has never been on air. All measured delivery numbers are for the SMALLER 10.304 ms frame, so a real drive frame can only do worse.
- image_rx_daemon.py:1083-1089 contains an in-tree note asserting the 171/1 soak was measured with the aligned pump DEAD. I could not confirm or refute that from the logs — the harness does not record LIFETRAC_ALIGNED_PUMP in params.txt. If true, the 0/386 result may indict only the idle-drain path rather than the aligned pump; if false, it indicts the pump. This is the single largest open question and it is answerable with one line added to the harness.
- In-stream vs idle classification is mine, not the harness's: a base TX is 'in-stream' if it falls between two tractor 'frame seq=N done' events separated by less than 3x the run's median train interval. It is insensitive to reasonable changes in that threshold (the delivered-vs-not split is bimodal and unambiguous in every run), but it is a reconstruction.
- I did not verify the 2046 B/s record claim for 174656 independently; the tractor-side stats line reports goodput=2045.9 B/s at util=83%, which I took at face value.

### Implications

- The reverse slot did NOT cost the record. 174656 is the highest-goodput run in the archive (2050 B/s median, 2104 peak, util 83%) and it ran WITH tx_prepare_ahead=1 and train_gap_ms=40. The slot's isolated cost is +22 to +25 ms of dead air per train boundary; at the record run's 0.528 trains/s that is ~12 ms/s = ~30 B/s = 1.5%. At the 20 fps runs' ~2.9 trains/s it is ~64-72 ms/s gross, and the measured end-to-end step (123122 -> 133257) is -64 to -73 B/s = -3.5 to -4.0%, which is an UPPER bound because the runt-aware batching commit landed in the same step and partly offsets it. Do not quote a point estimate; quote 'at most -4%, and ~0 once batching is held fixed'.
- DISPUTE 2 resolves in the critic's favour, harder than they argued. The per-fragment gap is 4.95 ms, not 12.7 ms and certainly not 44 ms. A 15.424 ms control frame is 3.1x too long for it; even the actual 10.304 ms frames these runs sent are 2.1x too long. The 44 ms figure in the code comments (image_rx_daemon.py:1038, frame_format.py:34) is a 2026-07-25 per-frame host-turnaround measurement from the pre-batching pipeline and has been carried forward as if it were per-fragment. Any architecture doc citing 44 ms of per-fragment listening must be corrected.
- But the architecture conclusion survives, for a different reason than claimed: the reverse slot is per-TRAIN. The measured boundary window is 92 ms (5-frag trains) to 245 ms (17-frag trains) and all three candidate envelopes fit inside it several times over. The constraint is not width, it is CADENCE - the window opens 0.53/s in the record run and 2.2/s in the soak, so the control-latency floor is 1.87 s and 0.51 s respectively. Neither is acceptable for hydraulic drive, and 1.87 s is disqualifying for E-stop under any reading.
- The empirical downlink number is the one to put in front of the operator: 0 of 95, and 6 of 255. Whatever the airtime arithmetic says, the tractor did not hear the base during DTS streaming in either 30-min-class run. In 171138 the base was firing a median 77 ms after the tractor's train-done - nominally inside the reverse slot - and 97.6% of copies still failed. Before any drive-plane cost model is trusted, this gap between geometry (should work) and observation (does not) must be closed; candidates are base/tractor clock skew, the tractor's modem not actually being in RXCONT during the slot, or the base's host turnaround overrunning the slot. This is a bench experiment, not a design argument.
- The soak's headline convergence number does not mean the downlink works. 171138 logged 171 CONVERGED / 1 GAVE UP (the claim quoted in commit 75de0f45), but only 6 REQ_KEYFRAME copies ever reached the tractor. 61 of the 171 converged after 0 attempts - the pending was cleared by a keyframe that arrived on its own - and the remaining 104 cannot have been command-driven either, since only 6 copies landed. At least 165 of 171 'convergences' were spontaneous. The convergence counter measures the encoder's keyframe cadence, not control-plane delivery.
- The link is saturated 3.8:1, so there is zero queue slack to absorb control traffic. 73% of offered frames are dropped at the TX queue with qdepth pinned at max and drop_stale=0. Every ms/s of airtime given to control costs the full 2.47 B/s; there is no 'the queue will catch up' argument available. Conversely this means the image side is already throwing away 4.6 KB/s of offered payload, so a 3-6% haircut for a 2-3 Hz control plane is invisible to the operator - the frame rate is set by the link, not by the offered load.
- The redistribution insight - today's dead air is already enough for ~8 Hz control, if it were scheduled instead of pooled. The record run spends 170 ms/s idle: 40 ms/s in useless 4.95 ms slivers and 130 ms/s in 0.53 huge, rarely-opening windows. Restructure to one fragment + one 20 ms listening slot per cycle: cycle = 99.904 + 20 = 119.9 ms, duty 83.3%, goodput 247/0.1199 = 2060 B/s - statistically identical to the 2050 B/s measured today - while opening a guaranteed 20 ms window every 120 ms (8.3 Hz, deterministic, ~4.6 ms of margin over a 15.424 ms envelope). The cost is giving up depth-2 TX pipelining, which is exactly what produced the 4.95 ms mid-train gaps. This is arithmetic, not a measurement, and it is the single highest-value bench experiment to run next.
- Cadence/envelope guidance from the table: 2-3 Hz at the 15.424 ms envelope costs 3.7-5.6% of image goodput and is affordable today with x1 copies (7.4-11.1% with the x2 redundancy the control plane actually uses). 5 Hz at 25.664 ms costs 15.5% (31% at x2). 10 Hz at 20.544 or 25.664 ms cannot fit in the dead-air budget at all (205 and 257 ms/s vs 170 ms/s available) and must displace image fragments outright. If E-stop needs 10 Hz, the honest statement is that it costs roughly a quarter to a third of the image link.
- DTS is the stable regime for the self-heal loop; FHSS is not. At profile 2 the keyframe requester ran at 17-58% of its 12/min ceiling with <=0.2% reverse-channel duty. At profile 1 it ran at the ceiling and delivery collapsed 3.5x (pub 54 vs 191 with requests disabled) at identical TX goodput - the RS-4.14 self-interference pathology, confirmed on two independent pairs. Nothing in the 2026-07-26 DTS record shows a command-storm feedback loop, so the storm risk is not an argument against the shared-radio decision at DTS. It IS an argument against ever running the shared radio at FHSS with an active control plane.

### Caveats

- params.txt git_sha is the last COMMIT at run time, not the running code. Proven: runs at sha 34fb75eb record train_gap_ms=40 and kf_request_disable=1 in params.txt, but `git show 34fb75eb:.../run_live_radio_monitor.ps1` has neither knob - the working tree was ahead of HEAD. So run 125048 (sha c09fcfc4, params tx_prepare_ahead=1) most likely DID run prepare-ahead even though the committed c09fcfc4 daemon has zero occurrences of TX_PREPARE_AHEAD. This is why the reverse-slot A/B cannot be cleanly attributed and why I report a bound rather than a delta.
- The briefing's PHY figure is slightly wrong and I did not use it. '243 B body + 8 B hop header = 251 B = 98.62 ms' omits the 4 B 0xFE fragment header that is inside the 247 B TX_FRAME_REQ body (image_tx_daemon.py:184). The real on-air packet is 255 B = 99.904 ms. This matters: with 98.62 ms the apparent per-fragment dead time would be 6.2 ms instead of 4.95 ms. The 99.904 ms figure is corroborated by the fact that dt_med lands at 104.85-104.94 ms in every profile-2 run and by the code comment predicting a ~5 ms mid-train restart (image_rx_daemon.py:1036-1041).
- The 15.42 / 20.54 / 25.66 ms envelopes are hypothetical. Every command frame actually transmitted in these runs was a 2-4 B body (pack_command_frame = magic + opcode + args, lora_proto.py:968-974) = 10-12 B on air, and the firmware reported pkt_toa_us=10304 for all 115/115 copies in 174656 and 275/275 in 171138. So a 16 B ControlFrame has never been flown; the cost table is an extrapolation from the measured exchange rate, not a measurement of drive traffic.
- The alignment analysis (command TX ms-after-train-done) assumes the base and tractor wall clocks are synchronized. Both logs start within 150 ms of each other at run start, but skew is unquantified and could be tens of ms. The 171138 result (median 77 ms after train-done, still 2.4% delivery) is the one sensitive to this; the 174656 result (median 1225 ms, 0% delivery) is robust to any plausible skew.
- Run 163103 is excluded from all delivery comparisons: TX goodput looks normal (1843 B/s) but the RX published only 6 frames in 30 minutes with len_med=53 B - the receiver was on the wrong profile (the 'stale retained-profile pin' the next commit, 13a57e69, records as invalidating run V). Its TX-side numbers are usable; its RX-side numbers are not.
- 'goodput' is not delivered image payload. bytes_tx_ok sums len(body) of every TX_FRAME_REQ including 4 B fragment headers, runt fragments, and PARITY fragments (image_tx_daemon.py:893, :1020). In the record run parity was 1780 of 7857 fragments (22.6%). The three rates are: 2.470 B/ms of TX body bytes, ~1.95 B/ms of image payload offered to air, 1.538 B/ms actually published to the UI. The cost table uses 2.470 (TX goodput); scale by 0.62 if the parent wants the answer in delivered-payload terms.
- Run 133257's params.txt says reg_profile=1 but the run streamed entirely at profile 2 - the tx daemon logged 'radio_profile: switching 1 -> 2' at 18:30:54, thirteen seconds before the first non-zero goodput sample. I treat it as a profile-2 run; that is what makes the 123122 vs 133257 comparison possible at all.
- The 2026-07-25 NOTES.md states 'ToA(246 B @ SF7/BW500/CR4-5) ~ 86.5 ms'. Recomputing with lora_proto.lora_time_on_air_ms gives 96.06 ms for a 246 B on-air packet; 86.5 ms corresponds to ~220 B. I could not reconcile this and did not use any 2026-07-25 ToA figure. The ~54 ms and ~44 ms dead-time numbers in that document inherit the discrepancy and should be re-derived before being cited further.
- n is small for the 120 s runs: 10 steady-state goodput samples and 10-11 air_gap windows each. The dt_med figures are extremely tight (spread <0.15 ms across all runs) so the 4.95 ms result is solid, but the goodput medians carry roughly +/-25 B/s of run-to-run scatter - visible in the four identical 083837/084343/084712/092403 runs landing at 1781 B/s with 1802-1812 peaks.

## Reconciliation

**Probes mutually consistent:** False

THE THREE PROBES AGREE ON THE HEADLINE AND CONFLICT ON THE NUMBER THAT MATTERS. All three measure the same inter-fragment dead air (4.95 / 4.98 / 5.01 ms) and all three settle Dispute 2 against the architecture analysis. But probe 1 reports the record run at 88.6% duty with an 85-134 ms train-boundary window, while probes 2 and 3 report 83% duty with a ~240-245 ms window. That is a factor-of-2 disagreement on the only window a control frame can actually use, so I re-derived it.

PROBE 1 IS WRONG; PROBES 2 AND 3 ARE RIGHT. Probe 1 modelled each 17-fragment train as 16 full fragments + 1 runt (16*99.904 + 41.02 = 1639.5 ms). The real geometry has TWO runts. base_station/lora_proto.py:901-921 shows add_parity_fragments INTERLEAVES parity: for a 3000 B payload at parity_group=4 the train is [D1-D4]P1 [D5-D8]P2 [D9-D12]P3 [D13]P4, and group 4 holds only the 84 B runt, so P4 is a runt too. Train = 15 full + 2 runt = 15*99.904 + 2*41.024 = 1580.6 ms, i.e. 92.98 ms/fragment.
Two independent firmware-measured counters confirm this and refute probe 1:
  - bytes/fragment, from the daemon's own goodput and frags_ok deltas across 89 windows: median 230.1 B. Two-runt geometry predicts (15*247 + 2*88)/17 = 228.3 B. Probe 1's geometry predicts 236.5 B.
  - ms/fragment, from the daemon's util (accumulated from firmware TX_DONE time_on_air_us): median 93.44 ms. Two-runt geometry predicts 92.98 ms (0.5% match). Probe 1's predicts 96.44 ms (3.2% high).
Propagating the 3.2% error: probe 1 books 788.6 s of airtime where the firmware books 736.5 s. Residual boundary time = 889.9 - 788.6 - 37.0 = 64.3 s -> 133.9 ms (probe 1's figure, reproduced exactly) versus 889.9 - 736.5 - 37.0 = 116.4 s -> 242.6 ms (correct). Duty is 82.8%, matching the daemon's reported util=83%, not 88.6%.

Everything closes: 736.5 + 37.0 + 116.4 = 889.9 s. Probe 1's inter-fragment dead time and probe 3's airtime accounting ARE consistent with one duty cycle once probe 1's fragment geometry is corrected.

CONSEQUENCE FOR DISPUTE 2 - BOTH DISPUTED NUMBERS ARE WRONG AND THE FRAMING IS WRONG. The gap distribution is bimodal. 93.9% of gaps are 4.98 ms (nothing fits: not the 15.42 ms 26 B ControlFrame, not the 10.304 ms frame the base actually flew, firmware-reported pkt_toa_us=10304 in 115/115 TXs). 6.1% are the train boundary at 242.6 ms, where a 15.42 ms frame fits 15.7 times over. The "~44 ms" is a stale 2026-07-25 per-FRAME host-turnaround number (image_rx_daemon.py:1038 still cites it) promoted to per-fragment. The "~12.7 ms" is the arithmetic mean of the bimodal distribution and describes no gap that occurs. The correct statement: frame size is NOT the constraint - a 15.42 ms drive frame, a 20.54 ms D13-authenticated frame and a 25.66 ms GCM frame all fit the boundary identically, so crypto-profile shopping buys nothing. CADENCE is the constraint: one window per 1.85 s at record geometry, per 0.45 s at soak geometry.

Attempts histogram in radio_monitor_20260726_171138_13a57e69/rx_daemon.log: {0:61, 1:53, 2:21, 3:14, 4:7, 5:9, 6:3, 7:1, 9:1, 11:1, 20:1}, 171 CONVERGED / 1 GAVE UP, mean 1.608 attempts -> p = 0.62. DOES IT RECONCILE WITH THE GAP FINDING? Not fully, and the residual is the important part.
  - The 4.98 ms inter-fragment gap explains why commands never land BETWEEN fragments: 10.304 ms is 2.1x too long, 15.42 ms is 3.1x too long. That part is closed.
  - It does NOT explain 0/95 and 0/249, because the boundary window is 242.6 / 92.3 ms and the frame fits easily. Under random phase the expected per-attempt delivery is (242.6-10.3)/1850 = 12.6% -> 12 of 95 expected in the record run; under the alignment the base's pump is supposed to achieve it should approach 100%. Observed zero.
  - The record run is mechanically explained by PHASE. I bisected all 95 in-stream command TXs against the tractor's train-done timestamps: offset after the previous train-done p10=176, med=1225, p90=1570 ms in an 1869 ms period whose armed window is the first ~243 ms. Median 1225 ms into the train is roughly fragment 12 of 17: the base is firing squarely into the tractor's transmission, ~1 second from any window. Robust to any plausible clock skew.

The architecture analysis's window is real and is larger than it claimed; its cadence is 0.54/s, which is the number that disqualifies the current configuration for drive and E-stop, not the frame size.

The measured boundary is 65-243 ms depending on train geometry, i.e. 1.6-6x the designed 40 ms, so the slot sits inside pre-existing host turnaround and costs little or no throughput - the record 2050 B/s run had it enabled.
- One configuration did deliver in-stream: run J (084712, no parity, no prepare-ahead, no train gap, 65 ms window every 243 ms) delivered 14 of 24 copies = 58%, 3.2x chance. This proves the raw-opmode RXCONT re-arm can work during a train boundary, so the later 0/386 is not a broken-radio result.

### What they do NOT tell us

- Why the soak delivered 0 of 249 when geometry says it should have worked. There the pump IS clustered at the boundary (command-TX offset after train-done p10=68, med=77, p90=196 ms against an 87-92 ms armed window; 222 of 255 copies within 130 ms of train-done) and still nothing landed outside the fault stall. Random phase alone predicts ~20% delivery, alignment predicts near-100%, observation is 0%. This gap between geometry and air is the single unresolved fact in the archive.
- Which of the three changes introduced between run J (58% in-stream delivery) and the RS-3.10 runs (0/386) destroyed the alignment: parity_group=4, TX_PREPARE_AHEAD=1, or train_gap_ms=40. No run varies them one at a time, and the harness does not record LIFETRAC_ALIGNED_PUMP in params.txt, so it cannot even be established from the logs whether the base's aligned pump was live in a given run.
- One-way base-to-tractor command latency under load, on a single clock. Nothing in the archive measures it. The best available number is a 254 ms idle-link round trip in run 163103, which is dominated by the tractor's 0.25 s queue-poll timeout (image_tx_daemon.py:478), not by air.
- Cross-board clock skew, which is unquantified and demonstrably non-zero: in runs 163103 and 133257 the tractor logs a command dispatch 30-50 ms AFTER the base already has the resulting ack, which requires >=50 ms of skew. Every sub-100 ms alignment claim (including the soak's 77 ms median) is sensitive to this; only the record run's 1225 ms median is robust to it.
- Anything about a real drive frame on air. Every command flown yesterday was a 2-byte body (pack_command_frame, lora_proto.py:968-974) = 10.304 ms, firmware-confirmed at pkt_toa_us=10304 in 115/115 and 275/275 TXs. The 15.424 ms 16 B ControlFrame has never been transmitted; all measured delivery rates are for the SMALLER frame, so a drive frame can only do worse.
- Anything about drive or E-stop cadence. No run exercised more than ~3 Hz, and every opcode was REQ_KEYFRAME, ENCODE_MODE or RADIO_PROFILE. There is no latency tail, no jitter distribution, and no repeated-attempt statistics at anything resembling a control loop rate.
- Whether the boundary window is deterministic or merely typical. The 242.6 ms and 92.3 ms figures are run-averaged residuals; the raw per-boundary distribution is not archived because image_rx_daemon.py:1139-1145 emits only dt_med and dt_p95 per 10 s window. A hard-scheduled reverse slot would need the worst case, not the mean.
- Whether FHSS can carry a control plane at all. Profile 1 delivered ~1 of 701 in-stream and only ~42% even on an idle link, but no run isolates channel-hop misalignment from the general failure, so the FHSS number is uninterpretable beyond 'worse'.
- The opcode mix of the 94-95 undelivered record-run transmissions. The base's cmd_tx_ok/cmd_tx_fail counters do not record which opcode each frame carried, so a received-but-unlogged opcode cannot be formally excluded (it is excluded for REQ_KEYFRAME, ENCODE_MODE, RADIO_PROFILE and RADIO_PROFILE_CONF, which log unconditionally at image_tx_daemon.py:603-628).

### Decisive next measurement

A PHASE-SWEPT COMMAND-DELIVERY RUN. With the image stream at DTS saturation exactly as in radio_monitor_20260726_174656_75de0f45, have the base transmit a 26 B (15.424 ms) control frame at a COMMANDED offset from each train boundary rather than on reassembly completion, sweeping that offset in ~20 ms steps across the full train period, and count tractor-side receptions per offset bin. Instrument both ends to make the result unambiguous: on the tractor, log a monotonic timestamp at the moment RXCONT is actually re-armed (firmware/tractor_x8/image_tx_daemon.py:1073) and at the moment the next train's first fragment is submitted - that pair IS the true armed window, measured rather than inferred; and stream it back so the two clocks can be tied together, removing the >=50 ms skew that currently makes every sub-100 ms alignment claim unreadable. One 5-minute run yields P(delivery | phase) and the measured width of the armed window. That single curve settles everything still open: it tells you whether the 242.6 ms boundary is real listening time or an accounting artifact, it recovers the per-attempt delivery probability that the contaminated convergence counter destroyed, and it gives the worst-case one-way command latency the operator must accept before hydraulic drive or E-stop can ride this radio. If the curve shows near-100% delivery inside the window, the architecture is a scheduling problem and is solvable; if it shows 0% even at the correct phase, the single-radio decision fails on the air, not on the arithmetic. Two cheap add-ons make it conclusive: record LIFETRAC_ALIGNED_PUMP and the three RS-3.10 knobs in params.txt, and change image_rx_daemon.py:1133-1145 to log a raw gap histogram instead of median/p95 (a two-line edit).
